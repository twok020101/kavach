# Market scan: household insurance "protection layer"

Research for Kavach (Google AI Builder Cup, BFSI track). Checked 2026-10-14. Figures are as reported by the linked sources; where a source was thin I say so.

## 1. Bottom line

- **Nobody owns the whole job.** Every player does one or two slices: sell (Policybazaar, Turtlemint, ACKO), advise on a call (Ditto, Beshak), review one health policy (CoverSure, Ditto), store documents (Polifyx, Trustworthy, eIA repositories), or help at claim time (Insurance Samadhan). None combines *all household policies + family-context gap analysis across every line + a forward premium cash-flow calendar*.
- **Mix-and-match does not solve it today.** A user could combine an eIA (storage) + Ditto (advice on health/term) + CoverSure (health review) + a spreadsheet (premium calendar), but that is four logins, motor/travel/device cover falls through, and no single view reasons across lines (for example "your corporate cover ends if you switch jobs, and your term cover is below 10x income").
- **Standalone "policy vault" apps have a poor track record** (Knip, early PolicyPal). The ones that survived (Clark in Germany) monetise as a **broker**: the vault and gap check are the acquisition hook, commission on fixing the gap is the revenue. Kavach should be designed the same way, or sell B2B2C to employers/banks.
- **Rails are arriving that make aggregation cheaper:** India's Account Aggregator now carries insurance data, eIA repositories hold digital policies, and Bima Sugam (IRDAI's marketplace with a Public Insurance Registry) is slated for around November 2026. Singapore already has SGFinDex insurance data. This favours an *intelligence layer on top of rails* rather than another marketplace.
- **Regulatory headwind in India:** IRDAI's Sept 2026 proposals would cap term-life first-year commission at 25 to 30% (vs ~51% average today). Commission-only distribution gets thinner, which strengthens the case for an unbiased, low-CAC, AI-driven advisory model.

### India demand signals
- Insurance penetration stuck at **3.7% of GDP** in FY25 (life 2.7%, non-life 1.0%); density USD 97. [Cafemutual, Jan 2026](https://cafemutual.com/news/insurance/36646-insurance-penetration-stalls-at-37-again-density-rises-to-usd-97)
- Swiss Re: India's **health protection gap ~US$32bn** (2024), second in Asia after China; India saw the sharpest rise in household stress from out-of-pocket health costs. Asia total gap US$390bn (health 258, mortality 132). [Outlook Money, Jul 2025](https://www.outlookmoney.com/insurance/health-and-life-insurance-gaps-risks-are-growing-asis-gets-390-billion-wake-up-call)

This directly validates the "relying on corporate cover" gap. ### What this means for a global product
1. **The universal pattern is "public or employer base layer + private top-ups", and the gap sits in the seams.** India: GMC + retail health. US: employer medical + separate dental, vision, disability, supplemental. UK: NHS + income protection. Singapore: MediShield Life/DPS + IP/term. Australia: Medicare + super default cover. Kavach's core engine should model *layers* (statutory, employer, personal) per person, not product lists.
2. **Country = a rules pack.** Keep one reasoning engine (Gemini reads policies, asks questions, explains) and swap in per-country "rules packs": base layers, typical products, regulatory norms (e.g. 10 to 15x income for term in India; MediShield Life + DPS defaults in Singapore). This is a clean story for judges: India-first, Singapore/JAPAC next, global by design.
3. **Employer-cover dependence is a cross-border insight.** India's 45% GMC-only stat, US open-enrollment complexity, Australia's super-only life cover all say the same thing: people over-rely on cover they didn't choose and don't understand.
4. **Premium calendar generalises well**: annual-heavy (India) vs monthly (US/UK/AU) vs CPF-deducted (SG). Showing a 12-month cash-flow across all payers (salary deduction, CPF, card, EMI) is novel everywhere.
5. **Data rails differ, so start with document upload** (works in every country, and shows off Gemini document understanding) and position AA/eIA (India), SGFinDex (Singapore) and Canopy-style APIs (US) as connectors.

## 5. Positioning implications for Kavach

- **Category:** "AI protection advisor for the whole household", not another marketplace. Bima Sugam and Policybazaar own comparison and purchase.
- **Wedge:** Upload what you have (policy PDFs, GMC card, salary slip) and answer 5 questions; get a layered coverage map, prioritised gaps with rupee impact, and a 12-month premium calendar. No competitor does all three across lines.
- **Unbiased by design:** commission caps and "no spam" demand (Ditto's growth) say trust is the moat. Show the rules behind each recommendation.
- **Business model options (in order of fit):** (a) broker/corporate-agent licence with transparent commission on closing gaps (Clark model); (b) B2B2C to employers as a GMC companion and job-change continuity tool (ALEX model, India HR angle); (c) white-label for banks/AA FIUs; (d) premium subscription (Trustworthy model) is weakest in India.
- **Risks:** monetisation of pure vaults has historically failed; regulatory advice/licensing boundaries (IRDAI distribution rules, MAS FAA in Singapore); data access before Bima Sugam/PIR matures.
